The second Thinking with Images workshop, held on 1 September 2026, continued the experimental Show-and-Tell format developed within VORD2. Giovanna Targia, Stephanie Santschi, Martina Diaz, and Golnaz Sarka Farsi each introduced an image—or constellation of images—through which they explained an actual research problem, while Ana Zapata Guzman, Darío Negueruela del Castillo, and Iacopo Neri participated in the discussion, with Iacopo joining remotely. As in the other sessions, live hand-drawn diagrams attempted to capture not simply what participants said about images, but how images entered and redirected their thinking.

The cases revealed markedly different epistemic roles. Targia used Warburg’s Mnemosyne Atlas and the recurrence of a “gesture of despair” to examine the movement from visual similarity to historically warranted claims of transmission, emphasizing montage as a visualized argument rather than a classification grid. Santschi’s Hokusai print raised questions about landscape classification, culturally encoded places, poetry and image, and the productive mismatch between computational categories and Japanese visual conventions. Diaz’s Rococo furniture exposed an “interval of uncertainty” between description and interpretation: controlled vocabularies could identify motifs but struggled with relations, social meanings, and even the boundary between image, frame, and ornament. Sarka Farsi’s reading of Goya’s Third of May 1808 moved between personal familiarity, reproduction, close looking, affect, historical context, and the global circulation of an iconic image.

Compared with the first workshop, this session made the emerging methodology more explicit. Post-processing of recordings and diagrams began to distinguish recurring modes of thinking ON, ABOUT, WITH, and AGAINST images. A central takeaway was that productive visual research often occurs precisely when classification, description, or similarity becomes insufficient: uncertainty and mismatch prompt returns to the image, new contextual evidence, comparison, and reformulation of the research question.
We are grateful to all participants for their generosity in sharing expertise and their willingness to engage critically with our proposals. Documentation from the workshop — including session notes, slides, and preliminary schema drafts — will be made available in the coming weeks.
