The first Thinking with Images workshop seek to test and further develop  the exploratory Show-and-Tell format that would structure the later sessions. Catalina Salguero Palavicino, Ana Zapata Guzman, Darío Negueruela del Castillo, and Iacopo Neri participated, using individual images and image series to discuss not simply what they represented, but how visual material generated, complicated, or redirected research questions. At this stage the later vocabulary of thinking ON, ABOUT, WITH, and AGAINST images had not yet been formalized; the session instead provided some of the empirical situations from which that framework would subsequently emerge.
Catalina Salguero presented colonial Latin American images, including casta paintings, in connection with her research on representations of Afro-descendant subjects. Her project combines art-historical interpretation with computer vision while explicitly avoiding the treatment of race as a phenotypic classification problem. Instead, it examines historically situated visual codes such as clothing, posture, scale, compositional position, gesture, and objects, using computational retrieval and comparison as heuristic tools for identifying patterns, ambiguities, and social hierarchies.

Darío presented urban and domestic images associated with the Realistas de Madrid, including Antonio López’s Madrid desde Torres Blancas and works by Isabel Quintanilla. The discussion centred on how radical attention to a particular viewpoint, atmosphere, light, interior, or urban fragment can make a larger world perceptible—an issue that would later become central to his work on worlding, visual specificity, and computational models of cities. Isabel Quintanilla and Antonio López shared closely related practices of attentive observation, including painting together from Torres Blancas.


Ana Zapata discussed Juan Pablo Echeverri’s Miss Fotojapón, a decades-long accumulation of passport-format self-portraits. The work opened questions around seriality, repetition, self-representation, bodily transformation, identity, memory, and reception: the photographic record both documents change and actively provokes further acts of self-fashioning.
In retrospect, Workshop 1 already exposed a central VORD2 question: images do not simply illustrate research objects; instead, they organize categories, comparisons, memories, identities, and possible lines of inquiry.
